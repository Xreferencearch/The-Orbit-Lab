Brand Principles

Four Ideas,
One System

Click a principle to see how it works.

Farm to Table

On-site vertical growing turns the dining room into an active farm — herbs and greens harvested hours before they reach the plate.

The Growing Wall

Grown in the room, not shipped to it

Vertical towers wrap the dining room in living herbs and greens — the same plants guests see growing are the ones that land on their plate that night.

The Living Kitchen

A dashboard for every tower

Humidity, light, and yield are tracked live for every tower, so the kitchen harvests at peak freshness and builds the menu around what's ready that day.

Robotics & Motion

Conveyor & Arms

A conveyor circulates dishes through the space while robotic arms garnish and serve — motion and precision working the same line as the chefs.

Connected Guest Experience

The Orbit App

See what's growing, what's in your dish, and exactly where every ingredient came from.

Every Counter, One Line

One belt, any brand

The same kaiten line carries sushi, burgers, or bowls. Each activation brands the belt, the trays, and the signage — without touching the hardware underneath.

Full Traceability

Every dish, fully sourced

Point a phone at any dish on the line to see exactly which tower it grew in, how far it traveled, and what's in it — down to the gram.

Spatial Flexibility

Stackable, lightweight furniture and hydroponic walls on hidden wheels reconfigure the floor in minutes — the same space hosts a quiet lunch, a brand launch, or a full presentation by nightfall.

Built to Move

Stackable, light, gone in minutes

Every stool and table is light enough to lift with one hand and stacks flush against the next. A dining floor for two hundred folds down and rolls out of the way in minutes — the same footprint hosts a brand launch, a press briefing, or a quiet lunch service without a single wall moving.

The Garden Moves, Too

Hydroponic walls on hidden wheels

The growing towers that wrap the dining room aren't fixed either — each one sits on casters hidden inside its base, so the living wall itself can roll into a new position as the floor reconfigures around it.

One Floor, Three Events
Standard configuration — round tables seat a full dining service around the core.

Brand Integration
& Swappability

Kiosks reskin, table and space decor swaps, and signage reads different content — the same shell becomes a new brand for every activation.

Built to Reskin

One shell, a kit of parts

Canopy, header display, side signage, menu holder, mobility base — every kiosk is built from the same modular kit, so a brand can be swapped out in hours, not weeks.

Underneath the Skin

The interior kit stays constant

Prep counter, point-of-sale, storage, and service window are fixed in place and identical across every kiosk — only the surface-level branding changes when a concept swaps in.

Kiosk shell — same footprint, new skin per activation.
Table decor — signage, tableware, and menu cards swap per brand.
Retail shelving — the same merchandise wall, reskinned per brand.
Beyond Merchandise

Retail adapts to grab-and-go, too

The same modular shelving carries packaged meals, snacks, and drinks for a fast retail line — chilled and ready, stocked straight from the kitchen behind it.

Market Signal

Pop-Ups Move
Faster Than Menus

A cookie video, a canned sushi roll, a limited-run cheesecake — a format can go from a single storefront to a global craving in a week. NYC's stuffed cookies, Sushi To-Go's tube packaging in Paris, chocolate-dipped fruit skewers in London, Mickey-shaped pancakes with a topping bar: none of them needed a full restaurant build to prove themselves.

That's the gap the kiosk format is built to close. A trend this fast either gets tested inside a reversible module in days, or it's gone before a traditional build-out is even permitted.
